Two Sibling Fields, Two Different Lists

CommunityFeatures names pickleball. AssociationAmenities does not, and 29 of 235 markets report it there. Basketball and shuffleboard are the mirror image: standard on AssociationAmenities, absent from CommunityFeatures, where 18 and 14 of 270 markets report them.

On AssociationAmenities, pickleball reaches 29 of 235 markets and shuffleboard 27. Only shuffleboard has a standard value there.

Sport Court Is One of Three Broad Answers

Sport Court already covers the generic case and the Duplication Subgroup has approved it. It is a standard value of AssociationAmenities, where 32 of 235 markets report it, and of Amenities, a field added in Data Dictionary 2.1, where no market reports it yet. It is also reported on ExteriorFeatures and CommunityFeatures, which do not name it.

It is not the only one. AssociationAmenities has two more standard values for a court of no one sport: Game Court Exterior, sent by 24 of 235 markets and Game Court Interior, sent by 19 of 235 markets. So the field carries three broad answers, and a market can send any of them. A game court is also reported on CommunityFeatures, by 5 of 270 markets, which does not name it.

Some markets send another wording for the same idea, a general athletic court. It is not a spelling of Sport Court, so it is not counted in the row above: 2 of 270 markets on CommunityFeatures, 2 of 378 markets on ExteriorFeatures and 1 of 235 markets on AssociationAmenities.

Whether a named sport should be its own value or roll up to the generic one is the question the list above puts to the group.

Golf Is the Only Sport with a Place

Golf is in the rows above for the 2 standard values about playing it: Golf Course on AssociationAmenities, where 131 of 235 markets report golf, and Golf on CommunityFeatures, where 158 of 270 markets do. AssociationFeeIncludes does not name golf, and 24 of 242 markets report it there as something the fee pays for.

Golf has 7 more standard values, and none of them is counted. Of these, 4 say where a property sits, not a place to play: On Golf Course and Near Golf Course on LotFeatures, Golf Course on View and the same value on FrontageType. The other 3 are a picture of a course, a garage for a golf cart and a kind of property.

No other sport is named on LotFeatures, View or FrontageType. Markets report tennis on two of them: 5 of 369 markets on LotFeatures and 9 of 292 markets on View.

Sports Land on Fields for What They Cost

Tennis is reported on AssociationFeeIncludes by 40 of 242 markets, more than it reaches on 6 of the 10 other fields that report it. RentIncludes carries it too, for 7 of 144 markets. Neither field names a sport. What the fee or the rent buys is a separate question from what the property has.

Source. RESO Industry Aggregates, November 18, 2026 run, which holds 432 markets. A market is an organization that receives listing data, counted once from its latest certification report. Each count is the markets reporting that sport on that field in any spelling, out of the markets that report the field at all, which differs by field. The aggregates show markets per value, not listings.

Counting. Standard values are Data Dictionary 2.1. A value's display name and its Legacy OData value are one value, so TennisCourts and Tennis Court(s) are counted together: 153 of the 165 markets reporting tennis on CommunityFeatures send one of the two, and the other 12 send another spelling only. The table above, under Show the numbers, keeps the two groups apart for every count. A value that names paddle, platform or table tennis is not counted as tennis.

What is shown. The rows show fields with 2 or more markets, and every field that names the sport, which is in no count when it is below 2 markets. A further 32 sport and field pairs sit at a single market each, among them tennis on SecurityFeatures, PetsAllowed and ArchitecturalStyle, which look like reporting mistakes rather than vocabulary. Single-market pairs are set aside everywhere on this page: they are in no count above, in no tile and in no comparison. Croquet and soccer appear only at that single-market level, so they are not among the 13 sports. The Building Resource is for the real property itself, and listings carry that information in the Property Resource. Every row is a Property field except ImageOf, which is a Media field. Four fields are left out. Disclosures holds free text, and MLSAreaMajor and MLSAreaMinor hold area names: a sport named inside a remark or a subdivision name is not a vocabulary choice. BusinessType is left out because a batting cage there is a kind of business and not an amenity. Golf is one of the 13, on its values about play and the fee field only: see its note above.

Which sports. The page looks for 17 names, a fixed list of court, field and lawn games: pickleball, tennis, paddle or platform tennis, basketball, volleyball, badminton, racquetball, squash, handball, bocce, shuffleboard, croquet, table tennis, soccer, baseball or softball, sport court and golf. A sport is reported when 2 or more markets send it on one Data Dictionary field counted here, which gives the 13; badminton, croquet, table tennis and soccer do not reach that. Pastimes outside that list are not counted, among them bowling, ice skating and billiards. Golf is counted on its 2 values about play and on the fee field.

Fields of a market's own. Only Data Dictionary 2.1 fields are counted. Markets also send sports in fields they defined themselves, such as their own feature lists. None of that is in a row above. Golf is the largest case: 102 of the 432 markets in the run send a golf wording of any kind, place wordings included, in a field of their own. Tennis is next, at 88 of 432. No other sport passes 17 of 432.
